Method & sourcesfree images from Commons, credited one by one — and what the page doesn't do

The shields on this page are real images from Wikimedia Commons, chosen only under a free licence (CC BY-SA or CC BY). Each illustration is credited beneath it: file name, author (when identified), licence, and a link to the file's Commons page — which is authoritative for the author and the exact licence. The blazon remains a public-domain fact.

The public arms of Switzerland (the Confederation and the twenty-six cantons) are protected by the federal law of 2013 on the protection of coats of arms; yet its article 8 allows their use “as an illustration in a dictionary, a reference work, a scientific work” (our translation) — and that is the basis on which this page shows them (see, in the encyclopedia, the chapter “Germany and Switzerland”). Their blazons are translated from the French Wikipedia page “Armorial de la Suisse”.

An accepted consequence: this page isn't self-contained — the images load from Wikimedia. Two states (ancient Hungary and Burgundy) are still missing: I couldn't properly check their files, and I prefer to add them later rather than attribute blindly.

Starting from real arms. When the Atelier can read a whole blazon, its “Redraw it in the Atelier” button opens the Atelier on those arms: it redraws them in its own way, keeps the card's image alongside for comparison, and you can change them; it then says whether they are still the arms on the card. Under the other blazons, a line says where it stops — a word it doesn't know, a charge it can't draw, a phrase like “tenant” or “surmonté”: it doesn't guess what it doesn't understand, and “See where in the Atelier” shows the text there, highlighted where it stops. None of this is written by hand: every word the Atelier learns opens up the further cards it can read.

In English. The English names, blazons and notes on this page are translated from the French text on which the site is built; the drawing engine still reads the French blazon. Sources are named as they were read, whatever their language.
