Illustrated armorial

People

The coats of arms of people who really existed — kings, princes, captains. Here, real images of arms, each credited to its author and licence.

Method & sourcesfree images from Commons, credited one by one — and what the page doesn't do

Unlike the rest of the site (figures drawn from scratch), the shields on this page are real images from Wikimedia Commons, chosen only when they are under a free licence (CC BY-SA) and their author is identified. Each illustration is credited beneath it: author, licence and link. The blazon remains a public-domain fact.

An accepted consequence: this page isn't self-contained — the images load from Wikimedia. If a file is moved there, the image may go missing. That's the price of realism.

Starting from real arms. When the Atelier can read a whole blazon, its “Redraw it in the Atelier” button opens the Atelier on those arms: it redraws them in its own way, keeps the card's image alongside for comparison, and you can change them; it then says whether they are still the arms on the card. Under the other blazons, a line says where it stops — a word it doesn't know, a charge it can't draw, a phrase like “tenant” or “surmonté”: it doesn't guess what it doesn't understand, and “See where in the Atelier” shows the text there, highlighted where it stops. None of this is written by hand: every word the Atelier learns opens up the further cards it can read.

In English. The English names, blazons and notes on this page are translated from the French text on which the site is built; the drawing engine still reads the French blazon. Sources are named as they were read, whatever their language.
